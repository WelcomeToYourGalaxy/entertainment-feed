What this is. A feed on the entertainment industries worldwide, in twenty subjects, following the question the section it comes from asks: whoever controls the bottleneck controls what stories populations consume — Roman emperors, medieval churches, Hollywood studios, broadcast networks, platform algorithms. So the first subjects are the bottleneck itself and who owns it: film and the studios, recorded music, television and streaming, and the concentration figures the section cites. Then what decides which stories reach anyone now — recommendation engines, ranking, discoverability — and the tracking of individual viewers that feeds it. Then the part the section is most concerned with, measurement reaching inward: neurotechnology and emotion sensing, and the biometric data that flows onward from it. Then the hours taken, children's share of them, engagement design, the adult industry and its ownership, the people who make the work and what they are paid, synthetic performers and likeness, game monetisation, moderation, state control, local production, rights, and what is set against it. Nothing here is written or summarised: headlines and snippets are the publishers' own, every row links to the original, and no model touches the text.

This is a feed on the industry, not on its output. That distinction does most of the work here, because reviews, trailers, casting, box-office weekends, awards, recaps and celebrity coverage are nearly all entertainment coverage — and none of it is the subject. All refused. What the wire keeps is who controls distribution, what the algorithm promotes, what is measured about the audience, what the people making it are paid, and what the rules are. No term stands alone: "streaming" counts beside royalties, market share or a ruling; "algorithm" beside audit, ranking or suppression; "children" beside screen time, age verification or design code.

On the neurotechnology claims. The section quotes figures for emotion-sensing accuracy and advertising return that come from vendors and their own marketing, not from independent evaluation. This wire carries the reporting and marks what has a measured figure, but a company's claim about how well its own emotion recognition works is a sales claim until someone else tests it — and the research subject exists partly so that independent evaluations land beside those claims rather than behind them. The neural and biometric subjects are here because of what is being attempted and what data it produces, which is documented, not because the accuracy claims are settled.

Standing. Every wire is labelled by what kind of source it is, and every row carries the label. Regulators, courts & classification bodies is the bodies that rule — competition and data protection authorities, courts, and the boards that classify and certify. Research & industry analysis is the concentration, algorithm and screen-time research. Unions, privacy & audience groups is those arguing from the making and the watching ends. Press is general news across 26 languages. Standing is provenance, not a claim that a story is important.

Weight. Separately, each story is scored on what it carries: a decision — a ruling, a fine, a divestment ordered, a certificate refused, a law enacted, a strike settled (2); institutional material — a regulator or competition authority finding, an audit-office report, a peer-reviewed study (2); a measured figure such as market share, watch time, screen hours or a royalty rate (1); a pending decision with a date attached (1); a named jurisdiction (1); and a primary source (1). At three or more the row is marked consequential.

What is refused. Reviews, trailers, casting, box office, awards, recaps, celebrity and listings — nearly all entertainment coverage — along with sponsored content and market copy. The status line reports how many stories each harvest refused; on this wire that number is very large, and it should be.

Translation. The Translate control at the top renders the headlines and snippets on screen into any of 26 languages, through a free public translation endpoint — no account, no key, nothing stored. A translated row is marked as machine translation and keeps the publisher's original wording on hover, because a machine rendering is not the publisher's words. If the endpoint is unavailable the row simply stays in its original language rather than emptying. Translation applies to what is on screen; scroll further or widen a filter and press the control again.

Region. Placement runs three levels deep. Pick a region and a second row opens with its subregions; pick one of those and a third row lists the places inside it — Latin America to the Amazon Basin to the Brazilian Amazon, Oceania to Australia to the Northern Territory. Each row counts what it holds, so an empty subregion is visible rather than hidden, and every story files under the subregion and region above whatever place it names.

Limits worth knowing. The gate is mechanical: it reads words, not meaning. Standing is assigned per wire rather than per article. Google News caps a query at roughly 100 results over about 30 days. Coverage is uneven by language and the counts show it rather than hiding it.

Every wire read on the last harvest, with its standing: what it returned, and how many of its stories were refused. A wire that could not be reached says so rather than disappearing.
